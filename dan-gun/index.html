What Is Dan-Gun?

Dan-Gun is the second pattern in the ITF Taekwon-Do pattern system and follows Chon-Ji in a student's progression.

The pattern contains 21 movements and continues the development of fundamental technique, balance, direction, stance control and coordination introduced during earlier training.

As with every pattern, learning the sequence is only one part of good performance. Students should gradually develop stronger technique, better control, accurate positioning and confidence.

Meaning Of Dan-Gun
The Legendary Founder Of Korea

Dan-Gun is named after the holy Dan-Gun who, according to Korean legend, founded Korea in 2333 B.C.

Understanding the meaning behind each pattern helps students learn more than a sequence of movements and connects their technical training with the history represented within the ITF pattern system.

Training Dan-Gun

Building On Your First Pattern

Dan-Gun gives students another opportunity to develop the fundamentals learned while practising Chon-Ji.

01

Know The Sequence

Learn the complete pattern confidently before trying to increase speed or power.

02

Develop Strong Stances

Pay attention to stance dimensions, weight distribution and stability throughout the pattern.

03

Control Every Turn

Maintain balance and awareness when changing direction. Do not allow the turn itself to disrupt your technique.

04

Keep Good Posture

Strong posture makes techniques more controlled and helps prevent unnecessary movement between positions.

05

Use Breathing

Coordinate breathing with technique to support focus, timing and the development of power.

06

Practise With Purpose

Avoid simply running through the sequence. Concentrate on making each repetition technically better.

Grading Preparation

Preparing Dan-Gun For Grading

Know The Name

Be able to identify Dan-Gun and explain who the pattern is named after.

Remember 21 Movements

Dan-Gun contains 21 movements. Practise until the complete sequence can be performed confidently without following another student.

Maintain Direction

Good pattern performance requires awareness of direction and controlled movement throughout the sequence.

Improve Technique

Concentrate on the quality of each block and attack rather than simply remembering what comes next.

Keep Training Chon-Ji

Progressing to Dan-Gun does not mean forgetting earlier patterns. Continue practising Chon-Ji alongside your newer material.

Exact grading requirements can vary between Taekwon-Do schools and organisations. Always follow the technical instruction and syllabus provided by your own instructor.
