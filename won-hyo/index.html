What Is Won-Hyo?

Won-Hyo is the fourth pattern in the ITF Taekwon-Do pattern system, following Chon-Ji, Dan-Gun and Do-San in a student's progression.

The pattern contains 28 movements and continues the development of balance, stance control, coordination, direction and technical confidence.

As students move into Won-Hyo, they should continue improving the quality of each technique while keeping earlier patterns sharp and consistent.

Meaning Of Won-Hyo
The Monk Who Introduced Buddhism To Silla

Won-Hyo was the noted monk who introduced Buddhism to the Silla Dynasty in 686 A.D.

Learning the meaning behind each pattern helps students connect their technical training with the Korean history and figures represented throughout the ITF pattern system.

Training Won-Hyo

Building Better Control

Won-Hyo introduces a longer sequence and gives students another opportunity to improve control, precision and confidence in their pattern performance.

01

Know All 28 Movements

Learn the complete sequence until you can perform it confidently without relying on another student.

02

Check Your Stances

Pay attention to stance dimensions, posture, weight distribution and stability throughout the pattern.

03

Control Your Turns

Keep balance during changes of direction and avoid allowing the turn itself to rush the next technique.

04

Finish Each Movement

Give each technique a clear finishing position before moving into the next part of the pattern.

05

Coordinate Breathing

Use breathing with your movements to support rhythm, concentration and the development of power.

06

Keep Earlier Patterns Sharp

Continue practising Chon-Ji, Dan-Gun and Do-San while adding Won-Hyo to your regular training.

Grading Preparation

Preparing Won-Hyo For Grading

Know The Meaning

Be able to identify Won-Hyo and explain that he was the noted monk who introduced Buddhism to the Silla Dynasty in 686 A.D.

Remember 28 Movements

Won-Hyo contains 28 movements. Practise until the sequence can be performed confidently and independently.

Maintain Balance

Keep control during stance changes, turns and transitions. Avoid sacrificing balance for speed.

Improve Technical Quality

Focus on posture, stance, correct finishing positions, timing and the quality of every individual technique.

Review Earlier Patterns

Progressing to Won-Hyo does not mean leaving earlier patterns behind. Continue practising Chon-Ji, Dan-Gun and Do-San.

Exact grading requirements can vary between Taekwon-Do schools and organisations. Always follow the technical instruction and syllabus provided by your own instructor.
