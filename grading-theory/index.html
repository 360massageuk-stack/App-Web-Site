Preparing For A Taekwon-Do Grading

A Taekwon-Do grading is about more than remembering techniques. Students are expected to demonstrate progress in practical ability, understanding, discipline and knowledge appropriate to their level.

Good preparation means practising consistently rather than trying to learn everything immediately before grading day.

Your instructor should always be your main guide for the exact syllabus required at your school or association.

What To Revise

Core Areas Of Grading Preparation

The exact requirements vary by grade and organisation, but these areas form an important part of Taekwon-Do study.

01

Patterns

Know the patterns required for your grade, including sequence, movement count, direction and the meaning behind the pattern.

02

Korean Terminology

Revise common commands, techniques, stances, body sections and terminology used during class and grading.

03

Techniques

Understand the names and correct performance of the blocks, strikes, kicks and stances required at your level.

04

Theory

Study the knowledge your instructor has set for your grade, including Taekwon-Do principles, pattern information and technical understanding.

05

Sparring

Develop control, movement, awareness and the type of sparring appropriate to your level and syllabus.

06

Fitness & Flexibility

Arrive prepared physically. Regular training, mobility and conditioning help students perform techniques with confidence.

Study Smarter

How To Prepare Before Grading Day

Start Early

Spend a little time on theory every week rather than trying to memorise everything at the last minute.

Practise Your Patterns

Work on accuracy first. Once the sequence is secure, concentrate on balance, power, timing, breathing and technique.

Test Yourself

Use questions and quizzes instead of simply reading notes. Being able to recall information without looking at the answer is much stronger preparation.

Use Korean Terminology Regularly

Learn terminology as part of normal training so that commands and technical names become familiar rather than something remembered only for grading day.

Ask Your Instructor

If you are unsure about a requirement, technique or piece of theory, check it before the grading rather than guessing.

Grading syllabuses and terminology can differ between Taekwon-Do organisations and schools. Always follow the requirements given by your own instructor or association.
